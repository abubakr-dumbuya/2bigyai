Thirty Years. One Family. One Kitchen.

The story of 2BigYai is the story of a dream carried across an ocean, built one meal at a time, and rooted in the soil of Brooklyn for three decades.

From Dakar to Brooklyn

In 1990, a young man arrived in Brooklyn from Dakar, Senegal with two things: a love for food and a dream. He had never worked in a restaurant. He had no culinary training. What he had was a grandmother named Aminata — known throughout Dakar for her exceptional cooking — and her most enduring lesson: food doesn't need to be spicy to be good. It's mostly about flavor.

After losing a job at a shoe store, he began doing what came naturally. He cooked. He delivered home-cooked lunches to neighbors, hair salons, street vendors, and office workers across Downtown Brooklyn. Tomatoey stews over rice. Grilled meats marinated with lemon and onion. The flavors of Senegal, carried in pots across the borough.

Word spread. Demand grew. And in 1995, together with his wife Rama, he opened 2BigYai on the corner of St. James Place and Fulton Street in Clinton Hill. He was thirty years old. He had never run a restaurant. And he built one of Brooklyn's most enduring culinary institutions.

"I remember my grandmother saying, 'Food doesn't need to be spicy to be good. It's mostly about flavor.' That philosophy is in every dish we make."
Papa Diagne, Founder

Three Decades in Brooklyn

From a single pot of stew in a Brooklyn apartment to one of the borough's most critically acclaimed restaurants — this is the journey.

1990
Arrival
A Dream Arrives in Brooklyn
Papa Diagne arrives from Dakar, Senegal, joining younger siblings in Brooklyn. After losing a job, he begins selling home-cooked Senegalese meals to neighbors and local businesses — delivering food on foot across Downtown Brooklyn. His grandmother Aminata's recipes guide every pot.
1993
Growth
A Catering Business is Born
Papa and his wife Rama formalize their home cooking operation into a catering business, serving schools, churches, businesses, and community events across Brooklyn. The Senegalese diaspora community becomes their earliest and most loyal base. Demand outgrows what a home kitchen can produce.
1995
Founded
2BigYai Opens
With the support of his siblings, Papa opens 2BigYai on the corner of St. James Place and Fulton Street in Clinton Hill, Brooklyn. He is thirty years old. He has never run a restaurant before. The menu is built around his grandmother's recipes: Tiebou Jeun, Yassa Ganar, Mafe Yapp. The dining room is small, warm, and always full.
1990s
Culture
A Stage for Brooklyn's Artists
The original 2BigYai becomes a gathering place for Brooklyn's creative community. The restaurant's stage hosts jazz musicians, spoken word poets, and community gatherings. A generation of young artists — including Yasiin Bey (Mos Def), Talib Kweli, and Erykah Badu — perform at 2BigYai before they become household names. The restaurant is part of a celebrated network of Black-owned cultural spaces in Fort Greene and Clinton Hill.
2000s
Recognition
Brooklyn Begins to Take Notice
2BigYai earns its first wave of critical recognition and media coverage. Celebrity regulars — including Akon, Sizzla, M1, Kevin Powell, and Isaac de Bankolé — become regulars. The restaurant is named Best Senegalese Restaurant in New York. Food writers and critics discover what the community has known for a decade: there is no place like 2BigYai in Brooklyn.
2011
New Home
2BigYai Moves to Bed-Stuy
After 17 years in Clinton Hill, 2BigYai relocates to 1168 Bedford Avenue in Bedford-Stuyvesant — its home for over a decade. The new location brings a larger kitchen, a stage, and a dining room decorated with West African art and textiles. The loyal customer base follows. New generations of Brooklyn residents discover 2BigYai for the first time.
2025
Today
30 Years. Still Cooking.
The Infatuation awards 2BigYai an 8.5 out of 10 — describing it as a must-visit Brooklyn staple with fantastic Senegalese food. The restaurant celebrates its 30th anniversary with a community dinner and book signing. Reviewers who first visited in 1999 are still coming. Their children are bringing their children. And the next chapter is just beginning.

"I want people to feel like they're at home when they dine here. This place belongs to Brooklyn as much as it belongs to me."

Papa Diagne · Founder, 2BigYai · Est. 1995

What We Believe

01
Flavor Over Everything
Our grandmother Aminata taught us that food doesn't need to be spicy to be good. Every dish we make is built around depth of flavor — slow-cooked sauces, properly marinated proteins, and ingredients treated with patience and respect.
02
Community First
2BigYai was built by and for the Brooklyn community. From our earliest days feeding neighbors from a home kitchen to 30 years of hosting artists, activists, and families — this restaurant has always belonged to the people who fill its chairs.
03
Authenticity Without Compromise
We have never chased trends or adapted our menu to what was fashionable. The Tiebou Jeun we serve today is made the same way it was made in 1995. Authenticity is not a marketing strategy — it is our identity.
